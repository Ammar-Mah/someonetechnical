Create a polished, production-quality landing page for **Someone Technical**, available at **someonetechnical.com**.

# Project overview

Someone Technical is an on-demand human technical support service for people building software with AI.

Tools such as Lovable, Bolt, Replit, Base44, Cursor and Claude Code make it possible for non-developers to create applications. However, builders often become stuck when they encounter deployment, domains, databases, authentication, security, payments, API integrations, backups, hosting or other production-related issues.

Someone Technical gives them access to an experienced human engineer who can join a one-to-one session, understand the project, explain the problem clearly and help resolve it.

The service is not a development agency, a freelancer marketplace, a course or another AI coding tool. The customer remains in control of the project. Someone Technical provides human expertise exactly when it is needed.

# Core message

The central idea is:

**When your AI-built project needs someone technical.**

The website should immediately make visitors think:

> “This is exactly what I need. I have built most of my application, but I am stuck on something technical and need a real person to help me.”

# Target audience

The primary audience includes:

* Non-technical founders
* Solo entrepreneurs
* Small-business owners
* Designers and creators
* Product managers
* Professionals building internal tools
* People creating applications with AI coding platforms
* Builders who have a working prototype but are unsure how to launch it safely
* Builders who are stuck and no longer trust the AI’s repeated suggestions

Do not talk down to these users or portray them as incapable. They have already built something meaningful. They simply need expertise in areas where experience and human judgment still matter.

# Brand personality

The brand should feel:

* Friendly
* Direct
* Clever
* Human
* Calm
* Technically credible
* Reassuring without being corporate
* Playful without becoming childish
* Modern without following generic AI-startup clichés

The writing should use plain language. Avoid unnecessary developer jargon, exaggerated promises and fear-based security messaging.

The name **Someone Technical** should be part of the personality. Use natural phrases such as:

* “You probably need someone technical.”
* “Good news. You found someone.”
* “Show us what’s happening.”
* “Let’s get you unstuck.”
* “You built more than you think.”
* “Bring the project. We’ll bring the technical judgment.”

# Visual direction

Create a distinctive, memorable design rather than a standard SaaS template.

The page should feel like a friendly technical control room combined with a modern human support desk. It should visually express the moment when someone stops fighting with an AI prompt and brings in a knowledgeable person.

Use a strong editorial layout with generous spacing, confident typography and unexpected but controlled visual details.

The design can include:

* A warm neutral or lightly tinted background instead of plain corporate white
* Dark, highly readable typography
* One vivid signature accent colour
* Small interface-inspired elements
* Animated status messages
* Cursor, terminal or system references used sparingly
* Human touches such as conversational notes, annotations or subtle imperfect details
* Rounded components where appropriate, without making everything look like generic floating cards
* Clear contrast between AI confusion and human clarity

Avoid:

* Glowing AI brains
* Robots
* Neon cyberpunk visuals
* Generic blue and purple gradients
* Stock photographs of developers
* Excessive glassmorphism
* Fake dashboards
* Walls of feature cards
* Overly technical terminal screens
* Visuals that make the service look like an automated chatbot

The website must clearly feel like a service operated by real people.

# Animation direction

Animation should be one of the memorable parts of the experience, but it must support the message rather than distract from it.

Possible animation ideas include:

* The hero initially showing a stream of increasingly unhelpful AI suggestions, followed by a human message that calmly identifies the actual problem
* A blinking status changing from “Still asking AI…” to “Someone technical joined”
* Technical problems appearing as tangled lines and becoming organised when human assistance enters
* A cursor repeatedly trying the same action before a human pointer arrives and resolves it
* Small interface labels changing from “stuck” to “understood” to “resolved”
* Gentle text reveals, scroll transitions and responsive hover interactions
* A visual handoff from AI-generated code to human review
* A subtle availability indicator showing that a real expert can be reached

Animations should feel smooth, precise and slightly playful. Avoid constant motion, excessive parallax or effects that reduce readability. Respect visitors who prefer reduced motion.

# Page structure

The page is short and shows rather than explains: five sections (1–4 and 9), at most 300 words in the body, drawings and motion where a list would otherwise be. A visitor who is stuck needs the offer, not its explanation.

## 1. Hero section

The hero must explain the service immediately.

Suggested headline:

**Your AI built the app. Now you need someone technical.**

Supporting text, one line:

**One-to-one help from an experienced engineer, for the parts your AI keeps talking around.**

Primary call to action:

**Get someone technical**

Secondary call to action:

**See how it works**

Include a friendly availability message near the primary action, such as:

**Bring the problem. You don’t need to know what it’s called.**

The hero is a bold dark band. Its animated drawing shows the transition from being stuck with AI to receiving clear human help: a tangled line straightens as someone technical joins.

## 2. Recognition section

Use a heading such as:

**Does this sound familiar?**

Show relatable situations:

* “It works in preview, but I don’t know how to put it online.”
* “The AI changed something and now login is broken.”
* “I connected Stripe, but I’m not sure it is safe.”
* “It keeps telling me to update an environment variable.”
* “I have users coming. Is this actually ready?”
* “I don’t even know what question I should be asking.”

End with:

**You do not need to hire an entire development agency. You may just need someone technical.**

## 3. How the service works

Three simple steps, each a drawing, a title and one short line:

### Show us where you are stuck

Tell us what’s happening, in plain words.

### Meet someone technical

An experienced engineer joins you, one to one.

### Leave with progress

Solved in the session where possible, or a clear next step.

## 4. Areas of support

Present the support areas in an engaging layout rather than a conventional grid of identical feature cards.

Include:

* Deployment and hosting
* Domains and email
* Databases and storage
* Authentication and permissions
* Payments and subscriptions
* APIs and integrations
* Security and secrets
* Backups and monitoring
* Broken builds and unexpected errors
* Production and launch readiness
* Architecture and platform decisions
* Understanding what the AI actually created

Each area is a drawing and its name, with no explanation beneath it.

## 5–8. Off the page: positioning, types of help, continuity and trust

These still describe the service — for the intake, the legal pages and any page that explains more later — but the home page does not carry them:

* **Positioning:** we don’t take your project away from you; we help you keep building it. More immediate than a freelancer, more personal than automated support, more practical than a tutorial, more accessible than a fractional CTO, more focused than an agency. Never attack developers, agencies or AI tools.
* **Types of help:** Help Session (the starting point), Launch Check, Technical Companion, Rescue and Implementation. No invented prices.
* **Continuity:** with permission, a concise record of the project’s tools, hosting, integrations, previous issues and decisions. Never credentials.
* **Trust:** no testimonials, customer numbers, logos, ratings or unsupported claims. The page carries three principles in one line under the final call: real engineers, plain language, you stay in control.

## 9. Final call to action

Use a confident but friendly conclusion.

Suggested headline:

**You’ve asked the AI enough.**

Supporting text:

**Show the problem to someone who can understand the project, explain what is happening and help you move forward.**

Primary call to action:

**Get someone technical**

Secondary note:

**You don’t need to diagnose the problem before contacting us.**

## 10. Footer

Include:

* Someone Technical
* someonetechnical.com
* How it works
* What we help with
* Book a session
* Privacy
* Terms
* Contact

Add a concise closing line:

**Human technical help for people building with AI.**

# Conversion flow

The primary call to action should open or lead to a short intake process.

Ask only for essential information:

* What are you building?
* Which AI building tool are you using?
* What are you currently stuck on?
* Is the project already live?
* Would you prefer guidance, hands-on help or are you unsure?
* Contact details
* Preferred session time

Make it clear that “I don’t know” is an acceptable answer.

The form should feel conversational and easy, not like a corporate support ticket.

# Copy requirements

* Keep headlines concise and memorable.
* Write like a capable human speaking to another capable human.
* Use humour sparingly and naturally.
* Prioritise clarity over cleverness.
* Avoid buzzwords such as “revolutionary,” “cutting-edge,” “empower,” “unlock” and “seamless.”
* Do not imply that every problem can be fixed in one session.
* Do not exaggerate security guarantees.
* Do not describe customers as clueless or technically illiterate.
* Do not present AI as the enemy.
* Do not fabricate social proof.
* Do not include technical stack or implementation details anywhere on the page.

# Experience requirements

The finished page should:

* Feel excellent on both desktop and mobile
* Load into a strong, visually complete hero
* Have clear and repeated calls to action
* Remain easy to scan despite the creative design
* Use animation without compromising speed or accessibility
* Provide clear feedback for all interactions
* Support keyboard navigation
* Maintain strong colour contrast
* Respect reduced-motion preferences
* Avoid horizontal overflow and awkward mobile wrapping
* Feel ready for a real business rather than like a design experiment

The final result should make Someone Technical feel like a new, obvious category:

**The human technical help line for people building software with AI.**
